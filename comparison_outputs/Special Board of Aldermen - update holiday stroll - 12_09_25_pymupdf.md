## T H E  H I S T O RY 

What began in 1995 as a small community gathering in Downtown Nashua has grown into New Hampshire’s second largest community celebration (neck in neck with daily numbers for the Deerfield Fair, surpassed only by Laconia Bike Week). The Winter Stroll, held on the Saturday after Thanksgiving, traditionally features a candlelit procession led by Santa and Mrs. Claus from City Hall to the tree, a formal tree-lighting ceremony, live performances, and a dozen or so vendors. The event regularly draws crowds in the tens of thousands (commonly cited around 30,000 attendees) and has the way the city launches the holiday season. 

Over the decades the Stroll has expanded from a simple procession and tree lighting into a full evening of programming across Main Street and (now) the Riverfront with ice rinks, live music, food vendors, and family attractions now accompanying the traditional candlelit walk. 

Like many large public events, the Stroll has adapted to changing circumstances and continues to evolve with new attractions and layout changes driven by community feedback and logistical needs. This adaptability is part of why the Winter Stroll remains such a longstanding and beloved Nashua tradition.

## 2 0 2 5  F E E D B A C K 

“I didn’t get to experience most of it but I heard mostly positive feedback from people in person. We did have less foot traffic this year. People seemed to enjoy that there were more activities for their kids… I liked that Canal Street was kept open to traffic. It make id a little easier for staff to get to work. I did not like that Main St closed (at least on our end) for over an hour before vendors even started setting up.” 

John Plourde, Manager, Peddler’s Daughter 

“We usually do the same numbers for the Stroll as the night before Thanksgiving. We were down a few thousand for the Stroll this year. We were crazy from 7-11PM then it died down. I think it’s the trend as younger people aren’t really drinking like they used to but also we didn’t get the little bump from the tree lighting we usually do. That being said, I think having things to do on Main Street is more important than people standing in line to drink. We were still at capacity for hours and we’re happy. It was also so cold that I assume people didn’t want to stand in lines at bars and went home - I don’t blame them.”
